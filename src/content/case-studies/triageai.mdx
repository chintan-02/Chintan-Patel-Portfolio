## Problem

Emergency intake is a safety-sensitive workflow. A model label is not enough.

Clinical notes and structured vital signs must be converted into reviewable information. Higher-risk signals need explicit escalation. Clinicians must remain the final authority. Predictions, corrections, overrides, and supporting evidence need to survive beyond the current screen so the full decision path can be audited.

TriageAI is therefore positioned as a **clinical decision-support workflow prototype**. It does not diagnose patients, replace clinician judgment, or claim production clinical validation.

## Solution

TriageAI supports two intake paths:

- direct structured patient intake
- free-text clinician notes processed through a deterministic Clinical Intake NLP Safety Layer

The NLP layer extracts demographics, symptoms, vital signs, safety cues, missing information, and evidence snippets. Extracted values are copied into editable fields, but prediction remains locked until a clinician confirms that the information has been reviewed.

After review, FastAPI builds the model features, runs the final LightGBM V2 ESI 3/4/5 model, applies transparent safety rules, stores the assessment, and returns confidence and class probabilities. The clinician can accept, override, or mark the case for further review. Reviewed NLP evidence and clinician actions are persisted in the audit workflow and included in the backend-generated PDF summary.

## Clinical Intake NLP Safety Layer

The NLP layer is deterministic and evidence-linked rather than an unrestricted medical chatbot.

It can extract:

- age and gender
- chief complaint and symptom list
- heart rate, blood pressure, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, and temperature
- configured safety cues
- missing vital-sign fields
- source-text evidence for extracted values

Every extraction requires clinician review. The UI does not automatically call prediction after extraction, and the backend does not treat extracted values as verified clinical truth.

Reviewed NLP audit metadata includes the reviewed status, source, extracted fields, safety cues, missing fields, evidence snippets, and decision-support disclaimer. The audit payload intentionally excludes raw note text, diagnosis, treatment recommendations, and NLP-generated ESI decisions.

The source dataset contains 366,890 emergency-department records and 972 columns. The final ESI 3/4/5 modeling subset contains 257,306 records.

The model was selected after comparison with XGBoost, Random Forest, a Dense Neural Network, an RNN, and a GRU. Selection considered weighted and macro F1, class-level behavior, ESI 5 performance, and unsafe ESI 3-to-ESI 5 downgrades rather than raw accuracy alone.

## Human-in-the-loop design

Human review is not a decorative confirmation step.

Before prediction, the clinician reviews and can correct extracted fields. After prediction and safety escalation, the clinician remains responsible for the final care-routing decision. Overrides require a documented reason and are stored with the assessment and audit trail.

This separates three responsibilities:

1. the NLP layer extracts reviewable information
2. the model estimates ESI 3/4/5 probabilities
3. the clinician makes the final reviewed decision

## Source-of-truth architecture

The React frontend does not own assessment truth.

FastAPI and the database remain authoritative for:

- assessments
- predictions
- clinician reviews
- audit events
- dashboard summaries
- report records
- official PDF generation

Mock data remains limited to explicit development and test fixtures. Browser storage is not used as the source of truth for real assessments.

## Safety boundaries

TriageAI does not claim to:

- diagnose patients
- autonomously assign final clinical priority
- replace clinical assessment
- operate as a validated clinical system
- guarantee patient outcomes
- infer missing values
- use NLP output without human confirmation

A real clinical deployment would require formal clinical validation, privacy and security review, governance, regulatory assessment, controlled rollout, monitoring, and organizational approval.

## Engineering decisions

### Why deterministic NLP?

The goal was structured extraction with visible evidence, not free-form clinical interpretation. Deterministic rules make supported fields, missing values, and failure boundaries easier to inspect and test.

### Why keep ESI 1/2 outside the classifier?

The final model is strongest and most precisely evaluated for ESI 3/4/5. Higher-acuity signals are handled through explicit safety escalation so the system does not overstate model support for rare, high-severity classes.

### Why persist reviewed evidence?

A checkbox only proves that someone clicked it. Persisting the reviewed fields, evidence snippets, safety cues, and missing-information state makes the review itself auditable.

## Current limitations and roadmap

Current limitations include local SQLite persistence, demonstration authentication, no production RBAC, no managed cloud database, no clinical validation, no production monitoring, and a deterministic NLP vocabulary that requires controlled expansion and testing.

Next steps are:

- replace the remaining screenshot slots with verified React captures
- harden authentication and role boundaries
- move professional deployment persistence to PostgreSQL
- add secure secrets and cloud storage controls
- strengthen monitoring, audit analytics, and model-observation workflows
- evaluate NLP or model challengers only when a measured benchmark justifies the added complexity

TriageAI demonstrates end-to-end ownership across clinical data preparation, multiclass evaluation, model-registry integration, deterministic NLP extraction, React and FastAPI integration, human-in-the-loop safety design, SQLAlchemy persistence, auditability, backend reporting, automated testing, and responsible-AI boundaries.
