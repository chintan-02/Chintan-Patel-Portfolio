## It is not one technique

Explainable AI is often described as a model feature: add SHAP or feature importance and the system becomes explainable.

That is too narrow.

After building three different systems, I found that explainability changes depending on who needs to review the output, what action may follow, and what kind of failure matters.

## TriageAI: explain the model and the workflow around it

For ESI care routing, the question is not only “why did the model assign this class?”

The clinician also needs to know:

- which intake values were used
- what came from free-text extraction
- which evidence snippets supported those fields
- which information was missing
- what each class probability was
- whether a safety rule changed the routing result
- who accepted or overrode the output
- what evidence was preserved afterward

## ResumeIQ: separate the signals instead of hiding them

ResumeIQ has a different trust problem.

A single resume score cannot explain whether the issue is parsing, keywords, semantic alignment, skill visibility, structure, or writing quality.

ResumeIQ therefore presents these as separate signals:

- baseline role classification
- keyword overlap
- semantic similarity
- matched and missing skills
- ATS-style structure and content estimates
- generic or incomplete wording
- targeted improvement guidance

The explainability decision shaped the product itself. The user receives components they can inspect instead of one supposedly objective hiring number.

## PolicyGPT: prove the evidence or refuse

A policy RAG system needs to answer a different question:

> Does the indexed evidence directly support this response?

PolicyGPT surfaces page citations, excerpts, retrieval diagnostics, answerability reasons, numeric consistency, direct-support state, and provider availability.

If evidence is insufficient, generation is blocked. If evidence is supported but the provider is unavailable, citation-only evidence remains available.

## The common thread

Across all three projects, the design rule is the same:

**Do not make the user take the system's word for it.**

- show clinical evidence and human decisions where safety matters
- decompose resume signals where users need actionable feedback
- cite the source or refuse where unsupported generation is risky

That is the practical meaning of “Intelligence you can trace.”

## What explainability does not guarantee

Explainability does not prove that:

- training data is representative or fair
- a model is clinically validated
- an ATS-style estimate reflects a real commercial ATS
- a cited document is current or legally authoritative
- a confidence score is a guaranteed probability of correctness
- a system is secure or production-ready

Explainability makes reasoning and evidence more inspectable. Reliability still depends on data quality, evaluation, governance, monitoring, human review, and clearly documented limitations.
